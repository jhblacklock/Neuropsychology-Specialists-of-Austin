For Referring Providers

Referral information for pediatricians, schools, and attorneys.

Two board-certified neuropsychologists (ABPP). Evaluations for children and adolescents. What to send, where it goes, and when you get the report.

Referral details

What to send

  • The referral question, stated as a question. "Is this ADHD or a language-based learning disorder?" is usable. "Neuropsych eval" is not.
  • Patient name, date of birth, and a parent contact number.
  • Relevant history: imaging and neurology notes, prior testing of any kind, school records and any current 504 plan or IEP, medication list, birth and developmental history where you have it.

No insurance information is needed. The practice is private pay and does not bill carriers, so families should be told the fee is out of pocket before they call.

Where to send it

Fax to 737-215-8710. Confirm

There is no dedicated referral portal or referral email address. Confirm

Questions before referring: 512-637-5865. There is no separate provider line that bypasses patient intake. Confirm

Turnaround

Three to four weeks from the testing appointment to the written report. Confirm

The child is seen for a consultation first; testing is scheduled at that visit. Time from referral to consultation depends on the calendar — call for current availability. There is no authorization step to wait on.

Report delivery

The report goes to the family at the feedback session. With written parental consent, it goes to you as well, and to any other professional involved in the child's care.

Consent comes from the parent or guardian, not from the referrer. Where a referral comes from a school or an attorney, tell the family what you have asked for.

Speaking to the clinician

A phone consultation with the evaluating clinician is available on request after the report has been delivered. Confirm

Referral questions we take

Development and learning

  • ADHD, learning disabilities, dyslexia, dyscalculia, dysgraphia.
  • Autism spectrum disorder.
  • Executive functioning difficulties, where school data is not explaining the gap.
  • A student not progressing, or one whose testing profile does not match their classroom performance.

Acquired and neurological

  • Traumatic brain injury and concussion, including return-to-learn planning.
  • Epilepsy, brain tumor, brain surgery, and the late effects of chemotherapy and radiation.
  • Genetic and chromosomal disorders, cerebral palsy, spina bifida, effects of prematurity.
  • Baseline and serial measurement in a known condition.

Not yet documented

The list of referral questions this practice declines is not published here yet. Confirm

Until it is, call 512-637-5865 before referring anything outside the categories above — particularly forensic and independent educational evaluation work, where scope has to be agreed in advance.
