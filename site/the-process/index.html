The Process

What happens, in order.

An evaluation at Neuropsychology Specialists of Austin is three appointments and a written report. You will know the length of each step, and the cost, before you agree to any of it.

The three appointments

Step one

Consultation

One appointment

The consultation is scheduled to gather relevant background information, answer your questions, and hear what you are actually worried about. You know the history — the pregnancy, the milestones, the point at which school stopped working — and that history determines which tests are worth running.

If further testing is warranted, the testing appointment is made at that visit. If it is not warranted, we say so, and you do not pay for testing your child does not need.

Step two

Testing and evaluation

Two to eight hours

Testing is conducted by a trained psychometrist. It runs two to eight hours depending on the referral question, the nature of the problem, and whether previous testing results are available. Breaks are taken whenever your child needs them.

It is behavioral testing — tasks, questions, puzzles, and paper. Nothing invasive, nothing that requires preparation beyond a normal night's sleep and breakfast. Most children find it long rather than frightening.

Step three

Feedback session

Three to four weeks after testing Confirm

The feedback session is held with you, and with your child where that is appropriate, to go through the results and the recommendations. You leave with a written report you can hand to a school district, a physician, or an attorney.

With your consent, other professionals involved in your child's treatment can attend the session or receive information about the evaluation.

Before you call

Two things are worth having ready. Neither is paperwork — this is a private-pay practice, so there is no referral or authorization to obtain first.

  • A specific description of what changed. "He stopped finishing tests he used to finish." "She has been asked to repeat second grade." That is more useful than a diagnosis someone has already offered you.
  • Anything the school has already done. Any prior testing, a current 504 plan or IEP, recent report cards, and the name of the teacher who raised it.
