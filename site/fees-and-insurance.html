Insurance, referrals, and authorizations

Three separate things. Only one of them is our job, and the other two decide whether we can schedule you.

Insurance

Many carriers cover neuropsychological evaluations. Coverage varies by plan, so call the number on your card and ask what your plan pays for neuropsychological testing, and what your deductible and coinsurance are.

Deductibles are due at the time of service.

Referrals

Call your insurer and ask whether they require a referral for neuropsychological testing. If they do, get it before you schedule.

Referrals expire, and they cap the number of visits. Both are on you to track — check the dates and the limits, and get a new referral before the old one runs out. We cannot see you without a valid one.

Authorizations

If your carrier requires authorization for an evaluation, we complete that paperwork after the consultation visit. That one is ours.

If the doctor you are seeing is out of network for you, you supply the necessary paperwork.

Authorization for the initial consultation visit is obtained through your regular doctor or specialist, not through us.

Payment

HSA and FSA plans, MasterCard, Visa, Discover, cash, and check.

What you are paying for

An evaluation is expensive because it is time. A testing day runs two to eight hours, and every hour of it is scored, interpreted, and written up by a board-certified neuropsychologist before you see it.

What you get is a written report specific enough to act on: a diagnosis where the data supports one, the accommodations or treatments most likely to work, and language a school district or an employer will accept.

You should not have to call to find out what something costs.
