Insurance and payment

The short version: we do not take insurance, and nothing has to be approved before your child can be seen.

We do not bill insurance

We are not in network with any carrier. We do not submit claims, and we do not provide superbills or other claim documentation.

Plan on the fee being an out-of-pocket cost. We would rather tell you that plainly now than have you find it out after a testing day.

No referral, no authorization

Because no carrier is involved, there is nothing to obtain before you can schedule. You do not need a referral from your pediatrician to book a consultation, and there is no authorization to wait on.

If a pediatrician, a neurologist, or a school has sent you, bring what they gave you. It helps us frame the referral question, but it is not a requirement.

HSA and FSA

HSA and FSA cards are accepted. Whether your plan treats an evaluation as an eligible expense is between you and the plan administrator — ask them before the appointment rather than at the desk.

How to pay

HSA and FSA cards, MasterCard, Visa, Discover, cash, and check. Payment is due at the time of service.

What you are paying for

An evaluation is expensive because it is time. A testing day runs two to eight hours, and every hour of it is scored, interpreted, and written up by a board-certified neuropsychologist before you see it.

What you get is a written report specific enough to act on: a diagnosis where the data supports one, the accommodations most likely to work in a classroom, and language a school district will accept.

You should not have to call to find out what something costs.
