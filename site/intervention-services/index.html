Intervention Services

Five programs for what comes after the diagnosis.

Testing tells you what has changed. These are the services that do something about it — relearning skills, working around what will not come back, and getting a school to act.

The five programs

Cognitive rehabilitation

$50 per 15-minute increment

Helps a child reach the most independent level of functioning they can. Two things happen at once: regaining skills that were lost, and learning to compensate for abilities that have been permanently changed — by an acquired injury, a degenerative process, or a longstanding cognitive difficulty.

Programs are built around your child's interests, because that is what children will practice. The most effective ones involve a parent who can carry the work into daily life.

Cogmed working memory training

$1,500 flat

This practice is qualified to deliver Cogmed. It is a set of working memory tasks done on a computer — at school, at home, or wherever the computer is — with the difficulty adjusted by algorithm as performance changes.

Eight exercises a day, 30 to 45 minutes a session, five days a week, for five weeks. Performance is tracked online and reviewed with a Cogmed Coach who stays in contact for the whole five weeks.

School advocacy

$300 first hour, then $75 per 15 minutes

A neuropsychologist attends your child's school meeting or ARD meeting. The job there is to make the testing results usable by the educational team and to help build a 504 plan or an IEP that reflects them.

Reports get misread. Someone who wrote the report, in the room, is harder to misread.

Cognitive and executive coaching

$75 to $90 per session, depending on frequency

Direct training in cognitive and executive strategies for children, adolescents, and young adults with learning differences and developmental disabilities.

What gets worked on: memory, organization, note taking, fluency, test preparation, study skills, and time management.

Family therapy and education

Educates the family about the child's neurological condition and builds problem-solving strategies for the conflict and stress it causes. It also works to increase positive interaction in the family, advocates for the family with other systems, and makes sure recommendations are carried out consistently — across settings and over time.

This service is not in the published rate list. Ask when you call. Confirm

All five are private pay, like everything else here. Fees in full.

Diagnoses these services are appropriate for

Acquired and neurological

  • Acquired brain injury: traumatic brain injury, concussion, stroke, brain surgery, brain tumor, and the late effects of chemotherapy and radiation.
  • Neurological disorders: epilepsy, spina bifida, cerebral palsy, and the effects of prematurity.

Developmental and genetic

  • Genetic disorders.
  • Chromosomal disorders.
  • Developmental disabilities: ADHD, autism spectrum disorders, and learning and executive functioning difficulties.

Most of these services follow an evaluation, either ours or one done elsewhere. If your child has testing from another practice, bring the report to the consultation.

Next step

Ask whether a program fits

Call the office and describe the diagnosis and what is getting in the way at school or at home. We will tell you which of these five is the right one, or that none of them is.
