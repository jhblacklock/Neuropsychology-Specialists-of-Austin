The Process

Three appointments, and a report you can hand to anyone.

You will know the length of each step before you agree to any of it.

  1. Step one — Consultation

    One appointment

    You give the background, ask questions, and say what you are worried about. The clinician decides with you whether testing will answer it. If it will, the testing appointment is made before you leave.

  2. Step two — Testing

    Two to eight hours

    In one day, with breaks whenever you need them. A trained psychometrist runs the tests. How long it takes depends on the referral question, the nature of the problem, and whether you have been tested before.

  3. Step three — Feedback

    Three to four weeks

    That is the time from testing to the written report Confirm. You and your family then sit down with the clinician to go through the results and the recommendations. With your consent, other professionals treating you can attend or receive the report.

Board Certification

What the credential actually requires

Both clinicians are board certified in clinical neuropsychology by the American Board of Professional Psychology. The practice has held ABPP membership in Clinical Neuropsychology since 2004.

Board certification is not required to practice neuropsychology in Texas, and it is not a membership you buy. ABPP has certified psychologists since 1947 and is the umbrella for 13 specialty boards, one of which is neuropsychology. It works the way medical specialty boards do. To be certified, a psychologist completes:

  • Specialty training and supervised experience in clinical neuropsychology.
  • A review of credentials by the board.
  • A written examination.
  • Evaluation of submitted work samples — real reports, judged by peers.
  • An oral examination.

Certification is peer acknowledgment of competence to practice in the specialty. It is the reason a report from this office carries weight with schools, insurers, and other physicians.
