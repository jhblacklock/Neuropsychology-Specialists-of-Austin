What we do

Evaluation

A diagnostic testing procedure that measures brain function. It shows what a neurological exam, an EEG, or brain imaging cannot: how well your child's attention, memory, language, and executive systems are actually working, and what that means in a classroom.

The Process

Three appointments, and a report a school will accept.

You will know the length of each step, and the cost, before you agree to any of it.

  1. Step one — Consultation

    One appointment

    You give the history, ask questions, and say what you are worried about. The clinician decides with you whether testing will answer it. If it will, your child's testing appointment is made before you leave.

  2. Step two — Testing

    Two to eight hours

    In one day, with breaks whenever your child needs them. A trained psychometrist runs the tests. How long it takes depends on the referral question, your child's age, and whether they have been tested before.

  3. Step three — Feedback

    Three to four weeks

    That is the time from testing to the written report Confirm. You then sit down with the clinician to go through the results and the recommendations. With your consent, your child's school and physician can attend or receive the report.

Board Certification

What the credential actually requires

Both clinicians are board certified in clinical neuropsychology by the American Board of Professional Psychology. The practice has held ABPP membership in Clinical Neuropsychology since 2004.

Board certification is not required to practice neuropsychology in Texas, and it is not a membership you buy. ABPP has certified psychologists since 1947 and is the umbrella for 13 specialty boards, one of which is neuropsychology. It works the way medical specialty boards do. To be certified, a psychologist completes:

  • Specialty training and supervised experience in clinical neuropsychology.
  • A review of credentials by the board.
  • A written examination.
  • Evaluation of submitted work samples — real reports, judged by peers.
  • An oral examination.

Certification is peer acknowledgment of competence to practice in the specialty. It is the reason a report from this office carries weight with school districts and with your child's other doctors.

Conditions we address

If the concern below is the reason you are here, an evaluation is likely to be useful. If you are not sure, say so on the phone and we will tell you.

  • ADHD
  • Learning disabilities
  • Dyslexia, dyscalculia, dysgraphia
  • Autism spectrum disorder
  • Traumatic brain injury and concussion
  • Epilepsy
  • Brain tumors, and the late effects of chemotherapy and radiation
  • Genetic and chromosomal disorders
  • Cerebral palsy and spina bifida
  • Effects of prematurity
  • Executive functioning difficulties
  • Other neurodevelopmental and neurocognitive disorders

Fees

Our rates are published, and we do not bill insurance

An evaluation costs $2,200 to $3,500, depending on the referral question. That one fee covers the consultation, the testing day, scoring and interpretation, the written report, and the feedback session.

This is a private-pay practice. We do not submit claims to carriers and we do not provide superbills or other claim paperwork, so plan on the fee being an out-of-pocket cost. Because there is no carrier involved, there is also no referral or authorization to chase before your child can be seen.
