## PART 2 — THE WEBSITE

### What "best in market" means for this category

Most neuropsychology practice sites are one of two failures: a WordPress
template with a stock photo header and no information, or a wall of clinical
prose with no way to act on it. Beat both by being the site that answers the
questions people actually arrive with, in the order they ask them:

  What is this and do I need it?
  Who are you and are you actually qualified?
  What will happen to me, step by step, and how long will it take?
  What will it cost and will insurance cover it?
  What do I do right now?

Design consequences:

- The process (consultation, testing, feedback) is the centerpiece of the
  home page, not a buried subpage. Three steps with real durations attached.
- Fees are published on a primary page, not hidden behind "contact us."
  Transparent pricing is the single strongest trust signal in this category
  and almost no competitor does it.
- Board certification is the differentiator. There are few board-certified
  neuropsychologists in Austin. State it early, explain what the credential
  requires, and do not let it read as a badge graphic.
- Referrals and insurance authorizations are the largest source of patient
  anxiety and wasted phone calls. Give them a designed, plain-language
  section rather than fine print.
- Every page ends with one clear next action. Phone number is always one tap
  away on mobile.

**Neuropsychological Evaluation**

A neuropsychological evaluation is a diagnostic testing procedure that
measures brain function. It provides information other procedures — a
neurological exam, EEG, brain imaging — do not. It uses behavioral tests that
measure cognitive strengths, weaknesses, and symptoms, and is sensitive to
functional variance in specific brain areas and circuits.

Domains measured: motor, memory (visual and verbal), sensory-perceptual,
language, executive functioning, attention, intellectual functioning. Also
assesses the effect of a neurological or medical problem on emotional
adjustment and quality of life. For students, a psychoeducational component
(reading, arithmetic, written expression) may be added to support school
planning. Specific areas depend on the concern, the person's age, and current
functioning.

Who should be evaluated — adults: establishing a diagnosis when mental
abilities have changed; determining the effect of a known condition and
planning treatment or rehabilitation; supporting return to work and home
after injury; educating family members about the condition and its
management; providing a sensitive measure of symptoms when the diagnosis is
unclear.

Who should be evaluated — children: identifying cognitive strengths and
weaknesses in children at risk for cognitive problems or developmental
delays; children not progressing in school or showing attention or behavior
problems.

Conditions addressed: traumatic brain injury and concussion, stroke, brain
tumors, epilepsy, Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple
sclerosis, dementia, ADHD, learning disabilities, dyslexia, autism spectrum
disorder, other neurocognitive disorders.

How it helps: identifies strengths and weaknesses to aid diagnosis and drive
specific treatment recommendations; predicts which treatments, services,
compensatory strategies, or accommodations will help; tracks development or
cognitive function over time or after treatment; anticipates which situations
will be difficult in future. Patient and family education is provided.
Referrals are made for psychotherapy, cognitive rehabilitation, or other
intervention as appropriate.

**The Process**

Consultation — scheduled to gather relevant background information, ask
questions, and raise concerns. The practice works collaboratively with the
patient. If further testing is warranted, the testing appointment is made at
that time.

Testing and evaluation — conducted by a trained psychometrist. Lasts two to
eight hours depending on the referral question, the nature of the problem,
and whether previous testing results are available. Breaks are taken as
needed.

Feedback session — held with the patient and family to discuss results and
recommendations. With consent, other professionals involved in treatment may
attend or receive information about the evaluation.

**Intervention Services**

Cognitive rehabilitation — helps an individual reach the most independent
level of functioning possible. Focuses on regaining lost skills and learning
to compensate for abilities permanently changed by acquired injury, a
degenerative process, or longstanding cognitive difficulty. Programs are
tailored to background and interests; the most effective ones involve family
members who can provide intervention in daily life.

Cogmed working memory training — the practice is qualified to deliver Cogmed.
A set of working memory tasks performed on a computer at school, home, or
elsewhere, with difficulty adjusted by algorithm. Eight exercises daily,
30–45 minutes per session, five days a week for five weeks. Performance is
tracked online and reviewed with a Cogmed Coach who stays in contact
throughout.

School advocacy — a neuropsychologist attends the child's school or ARD
meeting to help the educational team understand testing results and build a
504 plan or IEP.

Cognitive and executive coaching — direct training of cognitive and executive
strategies for children, adolescents, and young adults with learning
differences and developmental disabilities. Targets memory, organization,
note taking, fluency, test preparation, study skills, time management.

Family therapy and education — educates the family about the relative's
neurological condition, builds problem-solving strategies for the conflict
and stress it causes, increases positive family interaction, advocates for
the family with other systems, and ensures recommendations are carried out
consistently across settings and over time.

Appropriate diagnoses for intervention: acquired brain injury (TBI,
concussion, stroke, brain surgery, brain tumor, chemotherapy and radiation
late effects), genetic disorders, chromosomal disorders, neurological
disorders (epilepsy, spina bifida, cerebral palsy, effects of prematurity),
disorders of aging (MCI), developmental disabilities (ADHD, autism spectrum
disorders, learning and executive functioning difficulties).

On board certification: ABPP has been the board-certifying body in psychology
